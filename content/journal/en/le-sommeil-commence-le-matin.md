Feeling slightly out of sync since the clocks went back? That is normal, and it is temporary. Your body runs on a 24-hour rhythm, and it has one favourite cue to set the time: morning light. As soon as daylight reaches your eyes, your body knows the day has begun. It switches on its daytime rhythm and, quietly, starts preparing the coming night. The sleep cycle starts... in the morning.

## Before any remedy, the basics

Before deciding there is a sleep problem, we invite you to check that the basic conditions are in place. It is less spectacular than a supplement or a gadget, and it is what changes the most.

**The bedroom.** Cool, between 17 and 19 °C. Dark, as close to complete darkness as possible: a blackout blind often settles the matter. Quiet, by tracking down the stray noises that can be removed. And no phone switched on.

**The schedule.** Regularity above all: going to bed and getting up at similar times, weekdays and weekends. The body likes trains that run on time.

**The plate.** A light evening meal, eaten early, two hours at the latest before bed. Tea and coffee remain stimulants: we keep them for the morning.

**Movement.** Moving enough during the day to build real physical tiredness, preferably outdoors. Intense sport in the evening, on the other hand, wakes you up.

## The phone goes to bed before you do

In the evening, a bright screen sends your brain a daytime message. Its bluish light holds back melatonin, the hormone that prepares the body for sleep. Yet the body needs around an hour and a half to two hours of darkness or very soft light to get ready for the night.

A few rituals we love: a set time when the phone goes to sleep, ideally an hour or two before you do; a charging spot outside the bedroom; a book, a gentle stretch or a real conversation instead of scrolling.

## Resetting the clock with light

When the rhythm has slipped, light remains the simplest lever. Here is the method we share during our stays, to keep up for three weeks:

- Get up at a set time and take in as much light as possible through your eyes within the hour that follows: outside, at least twenty minutes, even under a Belgian sky. In winter, every lamp on while waiting for daylight.
- Eat breakfast within twenty minutes of waking, without rushing and without exercise before eating.
- Skip naps during this readjustment period.
- In the evening, imitate the sunset: dim the lights and warm their colour, with a dimmer or candles.
- Leave sleeping pills and supplements in the cupboard: the aim is to find a rhythm again, not to force it.

## And if nothing moves?

If the foundations are in place and, after three weeks, things have not clearly improved, only then does it become useful to look further: digestion, stress, mental load, deficiencies. That is what the sleep consultation at the centre is for, looking at your nights in the light of the 7 axes.

No gadget, no complicated routine. Just light, at the right time. Your body knows how to adapt; it simply needs the right signals.
