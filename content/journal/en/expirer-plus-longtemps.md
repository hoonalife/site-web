A longer exhale is one of the simplest ways to tell your body it is safe. When the breath going out lasts longer than the breath coming in, the nervous system receives a calming signal. No equipment, no special place: a chair, a bench or your bed will do.

## Why it works

Our autonomic nervous system has two main modes. The action mode, which speeds up the heart and mobilises energy to cope. And the recovery mode, which digests, repairs and lets the pressure drop. Both are useful. What wears us down is staying stuck in the first one: full days, notifications, lists that grow longer at the end of the year.

Breathing is the only automatic function we have a direct hold on. On the in-breath, the heart speeds up a little. On the out-breath, it slows down, under the influence of the vagus nerve, the long nerve that links the brain to the heart, the lungs and the belly. Lengthening the exhale gives this natural brake more time. The body gets the message before the head does.

## The exercise: 4 counts in, 6 counts out

- Settle in, back supported, feet on the floor.
- Breathe in through the nose for a count of 4.
- Breathe out slowly, through the nose or through barely parted lips, for a count of 6.
- Repeat five times. No more, to begin with.

If 6 feels long, start with 4 and 5. If you feel light-headed, you are forcing it: go back to your normal breath. The intention is not to breathe a lot, but to breathe slowly.

## When to use it

Before a meeting, in the car at a standstill, in the supermarket queue. In bed, when thoughts go round and round. After a tense exchange. We like to slip it into three moments of the day: on waking, before lunch and at bedtime. Three times five rounds is less than five minutes.

> Tension is who you think you should be. Relaxation is who you are.
> -- Chinese proverb

At this time of year, the roles we hold at the same time multiply. Part of the tension in the shoulders, the jaw or the breath comes from there: the effort of being who we think we should be. A few slow breaths are sometimes enough to come back to yourself. Nothing to prove, nothing to fix.

## Stress does not only live in your head

It often shows up elsewhere first: shorter nights, a more sensitive digestion, energy that dips in the afternoon. These are signals worth listening to, not reasons to worry.
