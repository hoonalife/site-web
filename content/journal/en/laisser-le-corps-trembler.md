Watch an animal after a fright: it shakes from head to tail for a few seconds, then gets on with its life. That shaking is not leftover fear. It is how the nervous system discharges the adrenaline and cortisol mobilised to flee or fight. We have the same reflex. But we have learnt to hold it in: nobody shakes in a meeting, or in front of their children. The tension then stays stored in the muscles, and settles there.

## The psoas, the muscle of alertness

At the heart of this mechanism, a deep muscle links the lower back to the thighs through the belly: the psoas. It is the first to contract in the face of danger, to protect the organs. In many of us, it never quite lets go anymore. A tight lower back, stiff hips, short breath, light sleep: so many signs that the body still believes it is on alert.

> We are often more attentive to the smallest warning light on our car than to our own body. Spotting the first signs of imbalance lets us act before the vase overflows.
> -- Olivia De Stoop, founder of hoona

## What the TRE method does

Tension and Trauma Releasing Exercises, developed by David Berceli, is based on seven simple exercises. They gently tire the legs and the psoas, until the body itself triggers neurogenic tremors, those involuntary vibrations that start in the thighs and sometimes rise to the shoulders. Nothing to do, nothing to think: you lie down, let it come, and observe.

This shaking sends the brain the message that the danger has passed. The nervous system switches from action mode to recovery mode. People who practise regularly describe chronic stress that lightens, thoughts that spin less, falling asleep more easily, back and neck tension that releases, better recovery after exertion.

## What a session looks like

The first sessions are done with a trained facilitator, to learn how to dose. That is the important point: self-regulation is the key, the process is never forced. If the intensity rises too much, you lay the legs flat or turn onto your side, and the tremors stop. Ten to fifteen minutes of shaking are more than enough, two to three times a week. After that, the practice goes home with you.

After a session: drink water, rest, walk gently, avoid strong stimulation, screens included, and get enough sleep. The body integrates during the hours that follow.

## Who it is not for

The method calls for advice before starting during pregnancy, after an operation, with a recent injury, epilepsy, an unstable herniated disc or a serious heart condition. People carrying a heavy traumatic history practise only with supervision. In every case, TRE is part of a well-being approach and does not replace medical follow-up.
