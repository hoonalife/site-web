Movement is one of the 7 hoona vitality axes, and the simplest one to set in motion. No gym, no membership: a pair of poles, good shoes and a path. In Tervuren, the path starts at the door of the centre and enters the Sonian Forest.

## What Nordic walking does for the body

Done well, it engages 80 to 90 % of the muscle mass. Energy expenditure is 30 to 40 % higher than ordinary walking, with a release of endorphins but without the rise in cortisol that often comes with jogging. The poles spread the body's weight over four points instead of two: ankles and knees are spared.

With the technique, the deep muscles of the back grow stronger. The twisting movements massage the organs of the belly and give them more room. Opening the rib cage improves breathing; the dynamic stretches along the whole muscular chain support lymphatic drainage.

## What the forest does for the nervous system

Walking under the trees is not just about scenery. The calm their presence brings can be measured: the heart rate slows, cortisol, adrenaline and blood pressure drop, the nervous system finds its balance. Daylight sets the internal clock and supports vitamin D. And since it is hard to walk with poles while your mind is elsewhere, the practice brings you back into the body, the posture, the breath.

## Getting started

- **The poles.** Light but stable, at the right height: arm bent, slightly below a right angle.
- **The shoes.** A sole that grips, a free ankle for a wide stride.
- **The clothes.** Trousers that do not rub, layers you can take off: you warm up fast. A small bag with a water bottle.
- **The ground.** To begin, unpaved, flat and fairly wide paths. The Sonian Forest is full of them.

The movement is quickly learnt. After a few accompanied sessions you become independent, and the practice costs nothing more, apart from the poles. The essentials: synchronise arms and legs, engage the arms in a wide movement, keep the torso upright, breathe fluidly.

## How long

- Beginner: 20 to 30 minutes.
- Intermediate: 30 to 60 minutes.
- Optimal well-being: 45 minutes to 1 hour, up to 1h30 depending on energy and habit.

Recovery counts as much as the session. After the walk: drink, stretch gently, walk slowly to come back down, eat light and nourishing food, listen to what the body says. And do not go flat out every day.

## The rest of the week

Between two outings, a daily walk even a short one, a few posture exercises, gentle strengthening of the legs, back and arms, a little mobility for the shoulders and hips. Nothing heroic. The body likes regularity far more than feats.

At hoona, Nordic walking takes place in a small group on Tuesday mornings and Thursday lunchtimes, leaving from the centre, or in an individual session to learn the technique at your own pace. It suits almost everyone, at any age; only osteoarthritis of the hands and an acute flare of arthritis ask you to wait.
