Billions of bacteria live in our gut. They digest what we cannot digest ourselves, make vitamins, train our natural defences and talk to the brain all day long. At hoona, nutrition is the first of the 7 vitality axes, and the microbiome is its foundation: when it is doing well, energy, mood and immunity follow. When it suffers, you feel it in the belly first, then everywhere else.

Good news: it feeds on simple gestures, long before supplements.

## It starts in the mouth

The mouth is the first stage of the digestive system, and it already hosts more than 700 species of bacteria. Chewing slowly, putting the cutlery down between two mouthfuls, means handing the stomach a job already half done. Eating standing up, in a hurry, in ten minutes, is the opposite. A calm moment around the meal, even a short one, changes digestion. And a healthy mouth also means a gut less exposed to unwanted bacteria.

## Twelve habits that protect the microbiome

1. Savour each mouthful, chew slowly.
2. Eat sitting down, in a relaxed moment, with attention.
3. Prepare your own meals: simple, quick, delicious, nourishing.
4. Choose seasonal, local food, organic whenever possible.
5. Cook in stainless steel, wood or cast iron, with gentle steaming rather than high heat.
6. Prefer wholegrain flours and sourdough bread made from spelt, einkorn or buckwheat to refined products.
7. Eat fresh fruit between meals, as a snack, rather than as dessert.
8. Drink low-mineral water, filtered or bottled, regularly through the day.
9. Keep animal protein moderate and vary it: fish, poultry, eggs, dairy, red meat now and then.
10. Cook with coconut oil or ghee, dress with olive oil; leave refined oils aside.
11. Choose quality teas, discover herbal infusions, keep alcohol for special occasions.
12. Replace part of the chilli with fresh herbs: basil, coriander, thyme, rosemary, turmeric, oregano.

Nothing extreme. Each of these habits is taken up one at a time, and each one counts.

## What quietly damages it

Meals swallowed in a rush, ultra-processed products, sugar and refined flours, daily alcohol, and stress that never comes down: the belly is the first to pay. The microbiome and the nervous system talk to each other in both directions. A sensitive digestion in a tense period is no surprise, and it is often through breathing and sleep that we help the belly.

## And the fridge?

A detail we forget: cold slows bacteria down, it does not stop them. The upper shelves where liquids drip, the vegetable drawer and the handles are the busiest areas. Regular cleaning, leftovers covered and eaten quickly, raw vegetables kept away from raw meat: that too is digestive health.

## The vase and the overflow

The 7 axes work like communicating vessels. A tired microbiome weighs on sleep, mood and energy; restored sleep, calmer stress and a livelier plate recharge it. We invite you to start with one gesture from the list, the one that seems easiest, and to observe for two weeks. The belly answers quickly when you listen to it.
